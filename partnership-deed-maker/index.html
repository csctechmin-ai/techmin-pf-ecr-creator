Partnership deed maker for firms in India

A partnership deed records who the partners are, how much capital each brings in, how profits and losses are shared, who runs the business, and what happens when a partner retires, dies or leaves. This tool asks you about your firm and writes a deed under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, which you can download as a Word file or a PDF. It runs in your browser and your data stays on your device.

What you get

A deed with numbered clauses, schedules of capital and profit shares, signature blocks with two witnesses, checks on the main legal limits, and a checklist for stamp paper, signing and registration of the firm.

Review before you sign

The deed is a draft. Have a lawyer or chartered accountant review it, and follow the stamp law of your State.

1. Purpose

  • This tool helps you prepare a first draft of a partnership deed for a firm of individuals. You answer questions about the firm and the partners, the tool checks the answers, and it writes the deed. It is a drafting aid. It does not register the firm, buy stamp paper or give legal advice.

2. What the tool considers, and what it cannot do

  • The tool applies the main rules of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 (for example sections 4, 7, 9, 11 to 13, 16, 25, 30 to 33, 36, 40 to 44, 48, 58 and 69), the limit of 50 partners under the Companies (Miscellaneous) Rules, 2014, the capacity to contract under the Indian Contract Act, 1872, the tax rules for interest and remuneration to partners under the Income-tax Act, 1961 and the Income-tax Act, 2025 (interest at not more than 12 per cent simple, remuneration only to working partners, authorised by the deed and only from its date, within the limits of the law), the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, and the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 and the Registration Act, 1908 as pointers.
  • It also raises warnings on the Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act, 1950, regulated activities and professions, and foreign exchange rules (FEMA).

4. How the main checks work

  • Profit shares must total exactly 100. If losses are shared in a different ratio, those shares must also total 100.
  • Interest on capital is simple interest. A rate above 12 per cent is refused, because the excess is not allowed as a deduction.
  • Remuneration is for working partners only. Choose one method for the firm: a fixed amount a month for each, or shares of the most the law allows. In the first method the deed pays the lower of the fixed amount and the allowable amount.
  • A person under 18 cannot be a partner. A minor can be given a share of profit, and the deed explains the minor's position and the choice to become a partner within six months of reaching 18.
  • The firm name must not use Limited, Private, Pvt or LLP. A name with words such as bank, insurance or government gets a warning.
  • Bank signing authority: if the rule is joint operation, or single operation up to a limit, at least two partners must be ticked as authorised signatories. For the limit rule, enter the rupee amount (PD-E607, PD-E609).
  • The letters need the bank, the branch, the date of the letter and, for the resolution, the date and time of the meeting (PD-E601, PD-E602, PD-E608).
  • Spelling and grammar: the tool looks up each word in a dictionary of about 128,000 words, including Indian-English spellings such as authorised and organisation, and suggests the nearest correct word. Names, places and capital-letter short forms are not marked. It cannot judge meaning, so it does not replace a lawyer's review (PD-E701, PD-E702).

5. Stamp paper, signing and registration

  • A partnership deed is stamped under Article 46 of the Schedule to the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 as it applies in your State. The duty and the way to pay it (stamp paper or e-stamping) differ by State, so the tool does not work it out. Ask the State stamp office or an authorised e-stamping agent.
  • All partners sign, with two witnesses. Registration of the firm with the Registrar of Firms is optional, but an unregistered firm cannot sue third parties to enforce its rights (section 69).
  • The checklist you can download also lists the PAN, TAN, GST and other registrations to consider.

6. The documents the tool prepares

  • Partnership deed: 21 clauses, two schedules (capital and shares; remuneration and authorised signatories), a signature block for every partner and two witnesses on the last page.
  • Letter to the bank: asks for a current account, lists the partners with PAN, states joint and several liability, gives the mode of operation with a table for specimen signatures, an undertaking to report changes, and the documents enclosed.
  • Bank account opening resolution: follows the usual bank format. It records the meeting of the partners, opens the account, authorises the named partners singly or jointly, lets the bank honour instructions until it is told of a change, allows digital banking, ATM and debit cards, and says that a certified copy goes to the bank with the KYC documents. All partners sign it and the firm's seal is placed on it.
  • GST authorised signatory declaration: all the partners declare the authorised signatory for the firm under the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and the State or Union Territory Act, and the signatory signs an acceptance on the second page.
  • Execution checklist (free): stamp paper, signing, witnesses, registration and other registrations. Banks and the GST portal may ask for their own format; use these as the covering letter or the declaration and follow any format they require.

10. If something goes wrong

  • Every message shows a code such as PD-E301. Open the Error codes tab for the meaning and the fix. The same table is in the release notes PDF.
  • The deed cannot be created: fix the errors listed on the Check and download page (PD-E301).
  • A letter cannot be created: complete the fields marked in red on the Bank and GST letters page (PD-E601 to PD-E609).
  • The payment did not complete (PD-E510): if the payment failed, any amount debited is returned by your bank or Razorpay as their rules provide. Try again or use another method.
  • The payment could not be started or confirmed (PD-E511): check the internet connection and try again. If money was taken, the code is emailed to you.
  • The code is not valid (PD-E509): check the 12 letters and digits; letters O and I are not used.
  • The deed or Validate is greyed out after payment (PD-E515): this is by design. Choose Start a new deed to prepare another, which needs a new payment.
  • The Pay button is not active (PD-E513): select Validate, correct the errors, tick the confirmation and select Confirm.
  • The download limit has been reached (PD-E514): each document can be downloaded 5 times. Preview and the free checklist are not limited. A new deed needs a new payment.
  • Spelling or grammar notes (PD-E701, PD-E702): read the text shown. Correct a real mistake in the field named, or ignore a note for a name or a place.
